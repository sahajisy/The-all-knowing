# World, Places & Lifestyles / 世界の場所と暮らし

## Lifestyles & concepts from around the world
- **Hygge (Denmark)** — cozy togetherness; compare with Japanese こたつ culture
- **Lagom (Sweden)** — "just the right amount"
- **Fika (Sweden)** — coffee break as institution
- **Sisu (Finland)** — grit in the face of adversity
- **Ubuntu (Southern Africa)** — "I am because we are"
- **Pura vida (Costa Rica)** — a greeting and a way of life
- **Siesta (Spain)** — and why it's fading
- **Dolce far niente (Italy)** — the sweetness of doing nothing
- **Blue Zones** — Okinawa, Sardinia, Ikaria, Nicoya, Loma Linda; research claims are debated → health
- **Bhutan's Gross National Happiness** — measuring progress beyond GDP → economics

## Remarkable places
- **Svalbard Global Seed Vault (Norway)** — a backup of the world's crop diversity in the Arctic
- **Petra (Jordan)** — Nabataean city carved into rock; sophisticated water management
- **Angkor (Cambodia)** — vast city with hydraulic engineering
- **Socotra (Yemen)** — dragon's blood trees; one of the most biologically isolated places
- **Lake Baikal (Russia)** — deepest and oldest lake, ~20% of the world's unfrozen surface fresh water
- **Atacama (Chile)** — among the driest places on Earth; home to major observatories → science
- **Iceland** — geothermal energy, Althing parliament (930 CE)
- **Singapore** — city-state transformed in one generation; hawker culture on UNESCO list
- **São Paulo** — largest Japanese diaspora community in the world (日系ブラジル人)
- **Hawaii & Japanese immigrants** — plantation history, spam musubi

## Geography that explains the world
- **Why Europe conquered (Guns, Germs, and Steel)** — east-west axis of Eurasia; widely debated thesis
- **Monsoon** — rice cultures of Asia and the rhythm of life
- **Rivers & civilization** — Nile, Tigris–Euphrates, Indus, Yellow River
- **Chokepoints** — Strait of Malacca, Suez, Panama, Hormuz → economics: trade
- **Map projections** — Mercator makes Greenland look as big as Africa (Africa is ~14× bigger)
- **Time zones** — railways forced standard time (1880s); China uses one zone for the whole country
