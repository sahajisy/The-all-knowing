# Language & Linguistics / 言語・言語学

(The user is fluent in Japanese — use this for linguistics, etymology and cross-language
curiosities, not language lessons.)

## Japanese through a linguist's eyes
- **Three scripts** — kanji, hiragana, katakana (+ romaji); hiragana evolved from cursive man'yōgana
- **Man'yōgana / 万葉仮名** — kanji used for sound only, in the Man'yōshū (8th century)
- **Wasei-eigo / 和製英語** — "salaryman", "naitā" (night game), "pasokon"
- **Words Japan exported** — tsunami, emoji, karaoke, umami, tycoon (大君), honcho (班長)
- **Words Japan imported** — pan (Portuguese pão), arubaito (German Arbeit), ikura (Russian ikra), karuta (Portuguese carta), tabako
- **Meiji coinages / 和製漢語** — 経済, 社会, 哲学, 科学 coined or repurposed in Japan to translate Western ideas, then exported to Chinese and Korean
- **Keigo / 敬語** — language that encodes social relationships; compare with Korean and Javanese speech levels
- **Onomatopoeia / オノマトペ** — giongo, gitaigo; Japanese has an unusually rich sound-symbolic vocabulary (きらきら, しーん)
- **Pitch accent** — 箸 vs 橋 vs 端; regional differences (Kansai vs Tokyo)
- **Japanese & Korean** — similar grammar, but relationship is debated (likely not proven to be related)
- **Ryukyuan languages** — distinct languages, several UNESCO-listed as endangered; Ainu is critically endangered

## Language in general
- **Sapir–Whorf hypothesis** — does language shape thought? Strong version rejected; weak effects (color, direction) studied
- **Guugu Yimithirr** — an Australian language using cardinal directions instead of left/right
- **Pirahã** — debated claims about recursion and numbers
- **Universal grammar** — Chomsky vs usage-based linguists
- **Zipf's law** — word frequency follows a power law → technology: data, science
- **Language death** — roughly one language disappears every few months; ~7,000 languages exist today
- **Writing systems** — alphabets, abjads (Arabic), abugidas (Hindi), syllabaries (kana), logographic (kanji)
- **Hangul / 한글** — designed deliberately by King Sejong's court (1443); letter shapes depict mouth positions

## Etymology hooks
- **"Robot"** — from Czech "robota" (forced labor), Karel Čapek's play R.U.R. (1920)
- **"Algorithm"** — from al-Khwārizmī, a 9th-century Persian mathematician; "algebra" from his book title
- **"Salary"** — linked to Latin "sal" (salt), though the "Romans paid in salt" story is likely a myth
- **"Computer"** — originally a job title for people (often women) who did calculations
- **"Tycoon"** — from 大君 (taikun), used for the shogun in diplomacy
